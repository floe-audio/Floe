Parameters

Shorten parameter names​

In your DAW, the names of Floe's parameters are long and descriptive, such as "Effect Distortion On". You can switch to abbreviated names if you need the names to show in a smaller width, for example, "Dist On". Enable this option on Floe's preferences panel. You might need to close and re-open your DAW for this change to take effect.

Layer 1/ConfigMIDI Transpose On208L1 MIDI Transpose OnTranspose shifts this layer up or down in semitones by changing which samples get triggered, rather than by speeding the audio up or down. On a multi-sampled Instrument that keeps the character of the sound intact, so it's the better choice for large shifts. The key range isn't affected: you play the same keys as before, they just reach for different samples. Tip: the Pitch control at the top of the layer transposes by re-pitching the audio instead. That's the one to reach for on a single-sample Instrument, and Detune beside it covers amounts smaller than a semitone.
Layer 1/Playback/LoopLoop Mode209L1Lp Loop ModeSelect the Loop Mode for this layer. Floe can loop a portion of the sound for as long as you hold a note, so even a short sample can sustain indefinitely. Some Instruments come with loop points built in, while others let you set your own on the waveform. You can also turn looping off. There's 2 modes: 'standard' wrap-around loops jump from the loop end back to the start; 'ping-pong' loops bounce back and forth, alternating playback direction. The options that are available depend on the Instrument: library authors can provide built-in loops, allow or disallow custom loops, or require that certain sounds always loop.

Layer 1/ConfigPitch Bend Range214L1 Pitch Bend RangePitch Bend Range sets how far the pitch wheel bends this layer, in semitones. Each layer has its own, so for example, a lead can bend a whole tone while the pad underneath can be set to zero to ignore the wheel entirely. Bending re-pitches the audio by speeding it up or slowing it down, so large bends will noticeably change the character of the sound. With MPE enabled, this governs the wheel on the master channel; the per-note bend of each individual note follows the range the controller asks for.
Layer 1/ConfigMonophonic Mode215L1 Monophonic ModeMonophonic decides what happens when notes on this layer overlap. Left off, the layer is polyphonic and every note you play gets a voice of its own; the other two modes hold it to one note at a time, in different ways. It's set per layer, so a monophonic lead can sit on top of a polyphonic pad in the same preset. The arpeggiator handles its own notes, so this has no effect while the arpeggiator is running on this layer.
Layer 1/PlaybackPlay Mode216L1 Play ModeSelect the Play Mode for this layer: the engine it uses to play its Instrument. Standard Playback plays each sample straight through: the playhead moves steadily from start to end, with options for looping and reversing along the way. The granular modes instead rebuild the sound from a stream of tiny snippets called grains. In Granular Playback, the point grains are drawn from moves through the sample at a rate you set with Speed, giving a time-stretch-like effect. In Granular Fixed, you place that point yourself with Position, letting you freeze on one part of the sample and explore its texture.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularLength217L1Grn LengthLength sets how long each grain lasts, from a few milliseconds up to a second. Short grains chop the sound into a fine, buzzy texture that keeps little of the original's shape. Long grains preserve much more of the sample's natural character. Density and Smooth are both measured relative to Length, so changing it here changes how they feel too.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularSpeed218L1Grn SpeedSpeed sets how fast the playhead travels through the sample in Granular Playback mode. Grains are drawn from wherever the playhead is. 100% is the sample's original speed. Turn it down to slow the sound to a crawl or stop it altogether, or up to race through at up to eight times normal speed. The playhead still respects the sample start, looping and Reverse settings. In Granular Fixed, this knob is replaced by Position.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularPosition219L1Grn PositionPosition chooses where in the sample grains are drawn from, shown as the highlighted region on the waveform. Grains come from here for as long as you hold the note, so you can freeze on one moment of the sound and sustain it indefinitely. Tip: you can create movement with this parameter by setting the LFO's target to Grain Position, or by assigning this knob to a macro.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularDensity220L1Grn DensityDensity sets how many grains overlap at once, relative to their Length. At 0%, each grain starts as the previous one ends, so they follow on one after another. Turning it up starts new grains sooner, stacking more and more of them on top of each other. Low values give an open texture where individual grains can be picked out. High values pile up so many grains that they smear into a thick, continuous wash.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularSpread221L1Grn SpreadSpread widens the area of the sample that grains can start from. Each new grain begins somewhere between the playhead and a point up to this far past it, measured as a percentage of the whole sample. Small values keep every grain close to the playhead for a focused, precise sound. Large values scatter grains across a wide stretch of the sample.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularSmooth222L1Grn SmoothSmooth shapes the envelope of each grain: how gently it fades in and out, relative to its Length. At 0% grains start and stop abruptly, giving a hard, percussive edge. At 100% the fade-in and fade-out meet in the middle, so neighbouring grains blend into each other for a seamless texture.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularPan223L1Grn PanPan randomises where each grain sits in the stereo field. Every new grain is given its own position, picked at random within the range you set here. At 0% every grain plays dead centre. At 100% grains can land anywhere from fully left to fully right, spreading the cloud from one side to the other. A quick way to create an interestingly wide stereo image.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularDetune224L1Grn DetuneDetune randomises the pitch of each grain. Every new grain is nudged sharp or flat by a random amount within the range you set here. At 0% all grains play in tune. At 100% each grain can be up to a semitone sharp or flat. Small amounts thicken the sound like a chorus; larger amounts get progressively more smeared and out of tune.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularDirection225L1Grn DirectionDirection gives each grain a chance of playing the opposite way to the main playhead. At 0% every grain follows the playhead's direction. At 100% each grain is equally likely to play forwards or backwards. In between, you can dial in a blend of the two for meandering, less predictable tones.
Layer 1/Playback/GranularHarmony226L1Grn HarmonyHarmony gives each grain a chance of playing at a musical interval above or below the note you played, so a single note can bloom into a chord or a shimmering octave. At 0% every grain plays at the root. Turning it up shifts more of the grains, until at 100% every grain picks at random from the root and the intervals you've chosen. Choose which intervals are allowed with the Intervals menu next to this knob: pick a preset such as Octaves or Major Triad, or toggle individual semitones yourself.
